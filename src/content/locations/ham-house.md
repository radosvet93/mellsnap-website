Ham House is one of the most complete surviving 17th-century houses in Europe, and its gardens — meticulously restored to their original Stuart layout — are a genuinely extraordinary setting for portrait photography. The south facade of the house, with its deep red brick and formal symmetry, creates an architectural backdrop unlike anything else in SW London. In front of it, the parterre garden stretches in geometric precision, edged with lavender and clipped topiary.

The walled garden to the side of the house offers a different mood entirely — enclosed, sheltered, and intimate. In summer it's filled with kitchen garden planting, herbs, and soft climbing roses, creating a lush and romantic atmosphere that's particularly beautiful for couples portraits. In autumn, the vine leaves on the walls turn gold and the more structural elements of the garden come into their own.

Ham House's position right on the Thames adds another dimension — the riverside walk and meadows nearby give space for more relaxed, open-air portraits as a contrast to the formal grandeur of the house itself. Getting there via the Richmond riverside ferry in summer is half the adventure.
