York House Garden is one of the hidden gems of the Richmond riverside — a beautifully maintained public park that packs an extraordinary amount of visual variety into a relatively small space. The centrepiece is the Italianate water garden: a cascade of weathered marble nymphs and sea creatures tumbling down stone steps, entirely unexpected in a quiet Twickenham park. It's a genuinely dramatic and romantic backdrop for portrait photography, with a sense of faded European grandeur that photographs beautifully in almost any light.

Beyond the statuary, the garden opens into a series of distinct spaces — formal beds, a sunken garden enclosed by hedging, and a wisteria-clad pergola that comes into its own in late spring. Each section has its own atmosphere, giving a shoot here remarkable variety without needing to travel far between spots. In summer the planting is lush and full; in autumn the structure of the garden becomes more prominent and the colours warm considerably.

At the far end of the garden, the grounds open onto the Thames riverside meadow — a complete change of mood, offering open, airy portraits with the river and Eel Pie Island as a backdrop. The contrast between the formal grandeur of the Italian garden and the relaxed openness of the riverbank makes York House Garden one of the most versatile locations we shoot at in the borough.
